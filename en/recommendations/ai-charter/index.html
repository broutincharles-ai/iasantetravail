Shadow AI and usage charters: frame rather than ban

Employees already use AI tools that the organisation has neither chosen nor validated. This page explains how to spot them, what a usage charter should contain and what it does not replace.

By Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician · Updated on 13 November 2026

The four actions ↓What to put in a charter ↓

The essentials

Uses that are already there, to be framed.

The definition, then what a charter can and cannot do.

Direct answer

“Shadow AI” refers to uses of AI tools that are neither validated nor supervised, with in particular the risk of entering sensitive information into public chatbots.

A usage charter serves to frame these practices rather than ban them: it says which data must never be entered, which tools are authorised and who checks. It replaces neither the consultation of the works council nor the risk assessment.

Three signs that uses already exist.

The risk exists before any official deployment.

Weak signal

“I just wanted to save five minutes.”

A vague instruction, a personal tool and a copy-and-paste are enough for sensitive data to leave the intended framework.

Rule

No sensitive data in a non-validated tool.

The Présanse charter prohibits entering personal, medical or identifying data into a tool that the service has not validated.

03 / Draft

What should an AI charter contain?

The ten principles of the Présanse charter, turned into decisions. Written for French occupational health services, it presents itself as an adaptable template.

PrincipleDecision to write down
Human oversightIdentify who checks, validates, challenges and takes responsibility for the use.
Medical dataDo not send personal or health data to an unauthorised tool.
TransparencyDocument the tool, its purpose, its limits and the exact role of AI.
FairnessLook for biases and effects that differ between groups.
TraceabilityDocument uses, validations, errors, incidents and corrections.
GovernanceDefine who is responsible, the decision path and the conditions for stopping.
Proportionate useStart from a real need and choose the least intrusive system possible.
Security and reliabilityAssess the environment, access, errors and dependencies.
TrainingTrain before use and maintain professional skills.
Eco-responsibilityDo not call on AI when its usefulness has not been shown.

04 / Limits

What a charter does not replace.

Four points to deal with separately, charter or not. The references are to French and European law.

SubjectWhat to rememberOn this site
Works council consultationIn the case decided in Créteil on 15 July 2025, access to ChatGPT was governed by an IT charter: the use of the tools was nevertheless suspended until the consultation was closed. The Paris Court of Appeal upheld this on 21 May 2026: the fact that employees had already used these tools on their own initiative does not set the consultation aside.Créteil, 2025 · Paris, 2026

05 / For employees

Two habits before using a tool.

Drawn from the pathway for employees.

  • The tool is not authorised for this use? Enter no personal, health, confidential or identifying data. Instead, explain your need to your manager to obtain an authorised solution.
  • The tool is authorised? First check where the data goes, how long it is kept and whether it is used for training; for health data in France, certified hosting (HDS). Send only what is strictly necessary, with nothing identifying.

Pointers for employees

Key answers

Three frequent questions.

Short answers, with the page that gives the detail.

What is shadow AI?

Uses of AI tools that are neither validated nor supervised by the organisation, with in particular the risk of entering sensitive information into public chatbots. Présanse, the French federation of occupational health services, uses the term about those services.

Should ChatGPT be banned at work?

The pointers on this site go the other way: rather than a blanket ban, map existing uses, set out the prohibited data with concrete examples and offer authorised solutions. The aim is to understand practices and workarounds without driving them underground.

Does an AI charter remove the need to consult the works council?

The known French decisions do not point that way. In July 2025, the Créteil court suspended the use of AI tools whose access was governed by an IT charter, until the works council (CSE) consultation was closed. The Paris Court of Appeal upheld those orders on 21 May 2026.
