Essential

An information, training and prevention resource.

In occupational health, AI is both a professional tool and an organisational transformation. Its effects on workload, autonomy, skills, teams and health depend on actual use, objectives and deployment conditions — not only on the performance of the model.

AI & Occupational Health offers an open pathway to understand systems, identify risks, assess uses, organise prevention and govern deployments.

The specificity of occupational health

Understanding AI in occupational health is not only about learning to use it.

AI can be a tool used by a professional, but it can also profoundly change employees’ activity: expected productivity, organisation, workload, autonomy, skills, monitoring, management or cooperation can all shift independently of the model’s technical performance.

Two questions structure this site: what can the system do? and what does its use transform in real work?

04 / Method

A model never acts alone.

Technical performance is not enough to determine the value or the effects of AI at work. What matters is the sociotechnical system in which it is used.

  1. 01Modelcapabilities · data · limits · errors
  2. 02Usetask · user · oversight · accountability
  3. 03Organisationtargets · deadlines · rules · staffing · control
  4. 04Real activityworkload · autonomy · skills · teams
  5. 05Healthphysical · psychological · social effects

The same AI can produce very different effects depending on how it is introduced into work.
