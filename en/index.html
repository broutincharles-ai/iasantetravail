Starting point

Broaden what we mean by “safety”.

The AI safety debate still focuses primarily on model-level performance and risks — errors, bias, cybersecurity and misuse — and far less on the risks created when AI is deployed at work. Yet AI changes work organisation, autonomy, cognitive load, surveillance, jobs and skills. With nearly one in four workers worldwide potentially exposed to generative AI, AI safety must also become a shared concern for researchers, public authorities, employers, worker representatives and occupational safety and health professionals.

An international reference

The ILO estimates that roughly one in four jobs worldwide is potentially exposed to generative AI. Exposure does not automatically mean job loss: it calls for close examination of how tasks and working conditions are transformed.
