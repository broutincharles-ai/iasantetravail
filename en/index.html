01 — THE PROBLEM

We have expanded AI capabilities.
Not yet our definition of its safety.

AI safety brings together research and practices intended to make AI systems reliable, controllable and safe, from design through use. Yet the debate still focuses mainly on risks within the models themselves — errors, bias, cybersecurity or misuse — and far less on what happens when they enter an organisation.

At work, AI changes tasks, deadlines, room for judgement, responsibilities and skills. Its effects therefore depend not only on technical performance, but on how it is deployed, controlled and discussed. Broadening AI safety means examining these transformations before deployment and then in real activity: cognitive load, autonomy, surveillance, work quality, collective work and health. This perspective concerns researchers, employers, worker representatives, public authorities and occupational safety and health professionals together.

A model never acts alone.

05 — ABOUT

An occupational physician’s perspective on AI deployment.

AI & Occupational Health is an independent editorial initiative led by Dr Charles Broutin, an occupational physician and AI lead for the French Society of Occupational Health. The project connects the evaluation of AI systems with the analysis of real work: what tools enable, what they shift within an organisation and what must be verified to protect health.
