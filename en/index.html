What AI changes at work.
And how to prevent the risks.

An AI project also changes the work of the people who check its output. This independent site helps teams examine workload, skills and autonomy before deciding whether to expand a rollout.

Who checks the call notes?

Imagine a call centre where AI drafts a note after every conversation. The adviser compares it with the customer’s request, fills in missing details and approves the version colleagues will use.

Before shortening the break between calls, the team can time the review, record the errors and check whether each adviser can take control of a case.

What should you check before, during and after a pilot?

Involve the people who will use the tool when choosing it. Compare their work with how it was done before, then decide together whether to continue, make changes or stop.

Before choosingUnderstand & anticipateDuring the pilotObserve & adjustOver timeReview & act

Describe the work before choosing the tool.

  • Identify the tasks, responsibilities and teams involved.
  • Establish a baseline and involve workers.
  • Define the expected benefits, risks to monitor and review criteria.

Test the promises against work as it is actually done.

  • Measure checking time, errors and rework.
  • Check that people can correct, challenge or stop the system.
  • Adjust work organisation before extending deployment.

Monitor effects, not just adoption.

  • Compare workload, health and skills with the baseline.
  • Document changes in staffing, income and decision-making power.
  • Where possible, compare with teams still waiting for the tool to distinguish its effects from other changes.
  • Review benefits, address harm and suspend use if necessary.
The six requirements of the proposed framework
  1. Assess risks before deployment.
  2. Give workers an effective voice.
  3. Recognise hidden work.
  4. Ensure meaningful human control.
  5. Preserve skills and social support.
  6. Share benefits and monitor consequences.

A framework proposed in the article by Broutin and Faghri. Its effectiveness remains to be evaluated.

Which risks can we identify at work?

AI safety aims to prevent these systems from causing harm. The International AI Safety Report 2026 distinguishes malicious use, malfunctions and the effects of widespread adoption. Looking at work when a tool is first designed can help teams identify two potential effects of wider adoption.

Workers’ health

A faster pace of work, isolation or loss of autonomy can affect physical and mental health. If many teams face these conditions over time, the consequences could become a public health concern. The scale of any effects specific to AI remains uncertain.

Identify psychosocial risks →

Decision-making and bargaining power

As AI takes over tasks and decisions that draw on human skills, workers could lose influence over how work is organised and who shares in the gains. This kind of disempowerment remains a hypothesis at the scale of society.
