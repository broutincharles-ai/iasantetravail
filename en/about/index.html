BlueDot Impact · 2026
A stronger conviction
“Occupational health is now also an AI safety issue.”

Preventing the effects of artificial intelligence requires examining individual experience, the organisation of work and the systemic risks that could affect entire populations.

I completed BlueDot Impact’s AGI Strategy course to complement my work on AI-induced psychosocial risks, which had previously focused mainly on effects at the individual worker level.

Broadening the frame without losing sight of real work

Throughout the course, we collectively explored the different pathways through which advanced AI systems could cause harm, the potential scale of their consequences, the fragility of current safeguards and the time available to strengthen them.

Rapid advances in model capabilities and the rise of agents able to coordinate increasingly complex tasks mean that analysis can no longer be confined to the individual workplace, or even to a single organisation. Emerging signals concerning employment, particularly among early-career workers, reinforce the need for vigilance.

This reflection led me to consider these transformations as a potential major public health issue. Worldwide, hundreds of millions of workers could gradually face greater work intensity, increased emotional demands, reduced autonomy, deteriorating workplace relationships, value conflicts or greater employment insecurity.

An approach grounded in practice

This website, seminars, practical resources and my role as AI lead for the French Society of Occupational Health are already helping to structure this work and make it accessible to practitioners.

My research directions

Following the course, I decided to expand my work towards occupational-health-focused AI safety benchmarks, evaluations and red-teaming exercises, as well as fit-for-purpose governance frameworks.
