CSE and artificial intelligence at work: evaluate a project before giving an opinion

An operational framework to help the CSE obtain useful information, examine the effects of AI on work and make visible the missing guarantees.

Why it's crucial

Why should the CSE engage with AI at work?

Direct answer

Because an AI deployment doesn't just change a tool. It can redistribute decisions, objectives, verification burden, autonomy, skills, oversight and responsibilities. The CSE is the place where these transformations can be examined collectively before they become fixed in the organisation.

01

Intervene when the project can still evolve.

When consultation is required, it precedes the decision. The CSE can request information and guarantees as long as the choices remain open.

02

Examine actual capabilities.

An assistant can also access emails, files, data or software. The risk depends on the entire system, not just the intended use.

03

Make additional work visible.

Checking, correcting, handling exceptions, and recovering from errors consumes time. This load must be measured and recognized.

04

Organize collective monitoring.

The system evolves after its launch. Incidents, stopping thresholds and changes triggering a reassessment must be specified.

Jurisprudence 2026

Recent decisions have retained the obligation to consult the CSE for generative AI tools, including when their use was presented as optional.

Occupational health

Can AI at work cause psychosocial risks?

Direct answer

Yes, depending on its uses and its organisation. AI can help to intensify work, reduce room for maneuver, increase surveillance, weaken the meaning of work or shift responsibilities. These risks must be assessed based on actual work.

Points to examine with employees
  • Load, rhythm and interruptions
  • Autonomy and room for maneuver
  • Skills, quality and meaning of work
  • Monitoring and evaluation of activity
  • Collectives and working relationships
  • Evolution of professions and job security
Support that can be mobilized by the CSE

Seek the occupational physician’s opinion.

Advising staff representatives, the occupational physician can shed light on the possible effects of the project on health and working conditions. They participate in an advisory capacity in CSE meetings devoted to these subjects and can rely on the SPST multidisciplinary team.

01 / The minimal base

The 10 questions to ask before issuing an opinion.

For each question, indicate whether the employer's response is documented, partial or absent. The summary updates automatically.

01Purpose

What problem are we trying to solve with AI?

A precise need, the alternatives studied and the expected benefit.

02Capabilities

What can the system actually do?

Its available functions — not just the announced use case.

03Decision

What decisions can it make or influence?

Distinguish assistance, recommendation, rating and decision.

04Autonomy

What actions can it perform without human intervention?

Identify the actions performed, their scope and frequency.

05Access

What data and tools does it have access to?

Employee data, emails, files, internet and business software.

06Scope

Which employees and which professions will be affected?

Exposed populations, transformed tasks and specific situations.

07Actual work

What effects are expected on work?

Load, rhythm, autonomy, skills, collective, surveillance and employment.

08Recourse

How can a human challenge, correct or stop the system?

An identified person with the necessary time, information and authority.

09Incidents

How will unexpected effects be detected?

Reporting channel, logging, responsible and feedback.

10Suspension

Under what circumstances will the system be suspended?

Written shutdown thresholds and a tested rollback procedure.

02 / Power given to the system

Qualify the real autonomy.

The more the level increases, the more the justification, control, monitoring and reversibility must be robust.

Human control test

Who can stop the AI? Does this person actually have the time, information, skills and authority to do so?

Health–work signal

Is the project likely to modify the organisation, workload, autonomy, rhythms or working conditions?

Recommendation

This project presents potential impacts on working conditions. It is recommended to contact the occupational physician or the SPST multidisciplinary team before the CSE gives its opinion.

03 / The complete reference system

Seven blocks, in the order of a decision.

A short pathway that starts from the system, goes through the actual work and ends with a reasoned conclusion.

01

Understand the system

Model, vendor, version, hosting, memory, internet, tools and agents.

02

See the transformed work

Workload, autonomy, skills, working relationships, assessment and employment.

03

Qualify autonomy

From A0 information to A4 organisational delegation.

04

Prove human control

Time, information, skills, authority and real possibility of stopping.

05

Prepare for incidents

Reporting, severity, analysis, suspension and rollback.

06

Organize reassessment

New model, new data, new tools or a new population.

07

Formulate a conclusion

What is documented, what is missing and the guarantees to be strengthened.

04 / Essential answers

Frequently asked questions about the CSE and AI.

Direct answers on consultation, occupational health and the information to obtain.

Should the CSE be consulted before any use of AI?

Occasional individual use of an AI tool at the initiative of an employee does not, in itself, result in a consultation of the CSE. On the other hand, in companies with at least 50 employees, the introduction of an AI tool by the employer may fall under the prior information-consultation of the CSE, particularly with regard to the introduction of new technologies or its effects on the organisation and working conditions. Recent case law has thus required consultation for generative AI tools, including when their use was optional.

Specific rules

Certain uses are also subject to specific rules. In particular, the CSE must be informed in advance about recruitment-assistance methods and automated personnel-management processes, and must be informed and consulted before means of monitoring employee activity are implemented. Article L.2312-38 of the Labour Code ↗

Can the CSE consult the occupational physician during an AI project?

Yes. The occupational physician advises staff representatives on health and working conditions. When the introduction of AI is likely to change the organisation, workload, autonomy or pace of work, or expose employees to psychosocial risks, the CSE may seek their opinion. The occupational physician also participates in an advisory capacity in CSE meetings devoted to health, safety and working conditions.

Why should the CSE take on AI projects at work?

Because an AI system can redistribute decisions, the verification load, autonomy, skills, supervision and responsibilities. The CSE makes it possible to collectively examine these transformations before they become ordinary work constraints.

What information should the CSE request about an AI system?

At a minimum: its purpose, actual capabilities, the decisions it influences, its level of autonomy, accessible data and tools, the employees concerned, expected effects on work, human control, incident monitoring and suspension criteria.

Can an optional AI tool still concern the CSE?

Yes, depending on the circumstances. The optional nature is not enough to exclude any effect on working methods, workload, organisation or skills. The project must be qualified based on its capabilities, its scope and its real effects.

05 / Legal framework

A preparation tool, to be articulated with the applicable law.

Not every use of AI automatically triggers consultation. The workforce, the nature of the project, its capabilities and its effects must be assessed on a case-by-case basis.
