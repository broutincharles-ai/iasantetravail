CSE · AI AT WORK · OPERATIONAL FRAMEWORK

Assess an AI system before issuing an opinion.

A practical framework to help the Social and Economic Committee (CSE), the French employee-representation body, obtain useful information, examine effects on work and make missing safeguards visible.

By Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician · Updated 30 August 2026

01

Why should the CSE engage with AI at work?

An AI deployment does not merely change a tool. It can redistribute decisions, targets, verification work, autonomy, skills, monitoring and accountability. Where consultation is required, the CSE can examine these transformations collectively while choices remain open.

02

The ten questions to ask before the opinion

01Purpose

What problem are we trying to solve with AI?

A precise need, alternatives considered and the expected benefit.

02Capabilities

What can the system actually do?

Available functions, not only the announced use case.

03Decision

Which decisions can it make or influence?

Distinguish assistance, recommendation, scoring and decision.

04Autonomy

Which actions can it perform without human intervention?

Identify actions, scope and frequency.

05Access

Which data and tools can it access?

Worker data, email, files, internet and business software.

06Scope

Which workers and occupations will be affected?

Exposed groups, transformed tasks and particular situations.

07Real work

What effects are expected on work?

Workload, pace, autonomy, skills, teams, monitoring and employment.

08Appeal

How can a human challenge, correct or stop the system?

An identified person with the necessary time, information and authority.

09Incidents

How will unexpected effects be detected?

Reporting channel, logging, accountable owner and feedback.

10Suspension

Under which circumstances will the system be suspended?

Written stopping thresholds and a tested rollback procedure.

03

Qualify the system’s real autonomy

The higher the level, the stronger the justification, control, monitoring and reversibility must be.

04

Human-control test

Who can stop the AI? Does that person genuinely have the time, information, skills and authority required to do so?

Could the project alter organisation, workload, autonomy, pace or working conditions?

06

A legal preparation tool

Not every use of AI automatically triggers consultation. Company size, the nature and capabilities of the project, and its effects must be assessed case by case under the French Labour Code.

The CSE should obtain information before the decision where the project falls within its information-and-consultation remit. This tool supports preparation; it does not replace legal advice tailored to the situation.
