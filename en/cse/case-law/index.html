AI and the French CSE: the court decisions

French court decisions, from February 2025 to September 2026, on AI tools rolled out without consulting the works council (CSE) or without assessing their health effects. Each row names its source.

By Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician · Updated on 13 November 2026

See the decisions ↓The 10 questions before the opinion →

The essentials

What have French courts said about AI and works council consultation?

Three findings, as of 13 November 2026.

Short answer

Since February 2025, summary judges have repeatedly ordered consultation of the CSE and suspended AI tools rolled out without it. The Paris Court of Appeal confirmed this on 21 May 2026.

What triggers consultation is not the word “AI” but the project’s effect on work: a short, voluntary trial with no identified effect was not enough (Paris Judicial Court, 10 February 2026).

On 15 September 2026, an AI-based reorganisation was also suspended on the basis of the employer’s safety obligation.

The CSE (comité social et économique) is the elected employee representative body in French companies. In companies with at least 50 employees, it must be informed and consulted before new technologies are introduced. Article L. 2312-8 of the Labour Code (in French) ↗

02 / Reading

Five lessons for employee representatives.

What these decisions support, and the decision behind each point.

  1. The test is the effect on work, not the word “AI”.

    In Nanterre on 29 January 2026, the replaced tool already used AI: consultation was grounded on extension to all employees and on the use of data for appraisal and staffing. In Paris on 10 February 2026, the lack of an identified effect on jobs or organisation led the judge to rule it out.

  2. A “pilot phase” does not avoid consultation when tools are actually in use.

    In Nanterre on 14 February 2025, applications used by employees for several months were treated as implementation, not as a mere experiment. Conversely, a four-month voluntary trial with no rollout decision was not enough to require consultation (Paris, 10 February 2026).

  3. Optional or already spontaneous use does not remove the duty to consult.

    On appeal, on 21 May 2026, the Paris court held that making an assistant available on the intranet was likely to affect working conditions, and that employees’ earlier use of AI tools on their own initiative did not release the employer from consulting.

  4. A new version on the same technology does not necessarily call for a new consultation.

    In Paris on 2 September 2025, the new version of an HR chatbot, with no change of technology and no new feature likely to affect employees, did not require a new consultation. The generative AI platform opened to all employees did.

  5. Occupational health is a ground in its own right.

    In Créteil on 15 September 2026, the judge did not rule on consultation: a reorganisation was suspended because its effects on workload and health had not been sufficiently assessed in light of the employer’s safety obligation. According to ActuIA’s report, the risk assessment document mentioned AI-related risks without rating them.

03 / Reference points

Consultation: the rules to know.

The provisions these decisions rely on, set out on the French CSE and AI page.

Deadlines

One month, two with an expert.

Absent an agreement, the CSE is deemed to have given a negative opinion after one month; two months when an expert is involved; three months when expert assessments take place at central and establishment level.

Article R. 2312-6 ↗
Expert

A certified expert for new technologies.

The committee may call one in when new technologies are introduced or for a major project. The cost is shared: 80% for the employer, 20% for the committee.

Article L. 2315-94 ↗Ministry of Labour fact sheet (in French) ↗
Remedies

Summary proceedings and obstruction.

The summary judge can order the consultation and suspend the project. Obstructing the committee’s regular functioning is punishable by a fine of €7,500.

Article L. 2317-1 ↗
Occupational health

Safety obligation and risk assessment.

The employer protects workers’ physical and mental health and assesses risks; the CSE is consulted on the risk assessment document and its updates.

04 / Method

Sources and limits of this page.

What was read, what is taken from a commentary and what these decisions do not allow us to conclude.

Method

Each row points to the source consulted: the text of the decision where available, otherwise the commentary cited. The Nanterre order of 29 January 2026 was read in full. Case numbers and dates were cross-checked against the Éditions Tissot guide (in French) ↗ and ActuIA’s reference page ↗.

Apart from the Paris Court of Appeal ruling, these are first-instance summary orders: provisional decisions, made on the facts of each case, that do not settle the case law.
