Preventing the effects of AI on occupational health

A model can be technically reliable and still make work more intense, less autonomous or more uncertain. Assessment must include the effects of the deployed system on people.

AuthorDr Charles BroutinPublished14 AUG 2026
In this note01 · From the model to the work system02 · Health as a dimension of deployment

From the model to the work system

Model reliability, robustness, control and misuse are important technical dimensions. At work, they describe only part of the risk. AI enters an organisation shaped by targets, deadlines, power asymmetries and rules of responsibility.

The system actually deployed is the interaction between model, task, workers, organisation and implementation choices. Its effects on work cannot be inferred from a technical benchmark.

Health as a dimension of deployment

Cognitive load, intensification, surveillance, autonomy, prevented quality and employment insecurity can change without a spectacular incident. These gradual changes are risk signals when they durably damage health or the capacity to act.

Preventing the effects of AI on occupational health means observing these transformations before deployment and then in real activity, with workers and prevention professionals.

03

Why it matters

Workplace AI assessment is not limited to what a model does; it also examines what deployment does to workers.
