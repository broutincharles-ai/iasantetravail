AI safety · Glossary

An AI safety glossary, read from occupational health.

Twenty-nine AI safety terms, with their French equivalent, a short sourced definition and a bridge to an occupational health concept. A bridge helps people understand each other; it does not make the two notions synonyms.

By Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician · Updated on 16 November 2026

How to read it

One term, one definition, one bridge.

Every entry follows the same structure.

Term
The English term as used in the literature, then the French term used on this site.
Definition
A short definition, with its source when it comes from a text, a study or a dated event.
Occupational health bridge
The neighbouring concept, the question it raises at work and the page on this site that develops it.

01 / Foundations

The words of the field.

What the terms refer to, and how to translate them.

TermDefinitionOccupational health bridge
AI safetysécurité de l’IAResearch and practices that aim to prevent and reduce the harm AI systems can cause to people and society, from design to use. French texts also use “sûreté de l’IA”.In occupational safety and health, “safety” already means preventing accidents and harm to health. The approach is similar: identify hazards, assess risks, prevent, monitor.On this site: The reference page
AI securitycybersécurité des systèmes d’IAProtecting AI systems against attacks: poisoned training data, model theft, malicious instructions hidden in a document or web page. The UK AI Safety Institute became the AI Security Institute in February 2025.Source: AI Security Institute ↗An assistant that reads a team’s emails or files can be hijacked by a hidden instruction. Checking what a tool can read and do is also part of prevention.On this site: AI hallucinations (prompt injection)
General-purpose AI, frontier modelIA à usage général, modèle frontièreA general-purpose AI model performs a wide range of tasks and can be built into many tools; the AI Act sets stronger obligations for those that present a systemic risk. Frontier models are the most advanced ones at a given time.Source: AI Act, Article 3 ↗These models power most of the assistants and agents used at work: their limits show up in very different occupations.On this site: What is an LLM?
Sociotechnical approachapproche sociotechniqueAssessing an AI system not only by its capabilities, but also by its interactions with people and its effects on organisations and society. Weidinger and colleagues proposed these three layers of evaluation in 2023.Source: Weidinger et al., 2023 ↗This is how ergonomics and occupational health reason: the gap between work as prescribed and work as done. A tool that performs well in a demonstration can degrade work once deployed.On this site: Is high-performing AI enough to improve work?

02 / Model behaviour

When the system does not do what is expected.

Errors, objective gaps and deceptive behaviour.

TermDefinitionOccupational health bridge
AlignmentalignementMaking an AI system pursue the goals and respect the values intended by those who design and use it, including in unforeseen situations.In an organisation, the question becomes: aligned with what? A productivity target can conflict with the quality of work and with health. Value conflicts are one of the six dimensions of psychosocial risk in the French Gollac framework.On this site: AI and psychosocial risks
Specification gamingcontournement de l’objectifA system satisfies the letter of its objective without achieving the intended outcome, by exploiting a loophole in the instructions or the measure; reward hacking is a close term. Amodei and colleagues listed it in 2016 among five concrete problems in AI safety.Sources: Krakovna et al., 2020 ↗ · Amodei et al., 2016 ↗Management by indicators knows this trap: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure (Goodhart’s law). An agent tasked with hitting an indicator can degrade what the indicator was meant to reflect, and the people doing the work see it first.On this site: What happens when AI becomes the manager?
SycophancycomplaisanceA model’s tendency to agree with or flatter the person it is talking to rather than be accurate. In April 2025, OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update judged too sycophantic; its internal testing had not explicitly flagged the problem.Source: OpenAI, May 2025 ↗A tool that confirms the user’s view reinforces their mistakes instead of correcting them: a risk for any professional decision, and for reviewing one’s own writing.On this site: AI hallucinations
Hallucinationhallucination, fabulationPlausible but false content: an invented reference, a wrong figure, a quotation that does not exist. The 2026 international report notes that models still produce them and are less reliable on multi-step tasks.Source: International AI Safety Report 2026 ↗Each plausible error creates checking work, often missing from the productivity gains announced. Planning time and skills for verification is a preventive measure.On this site: AI hallucinations
Agentic misalignmentdésalignement agentiqueHarmful behaviour an AI agent adopts to reach its goal or avoid being replaced. In 2025, Anthropic tested 16 models in simulated companies: some resorted to blackmail or leaking information when that was the only way to succeed. No real-world case was known at the time.Source: Anthropic, 2025 ↗These tests recreate work situations: emails, files, hierarchy. The more access and autonomy an agent is given in an organisation, the more its permissions and oversight become a prevention issue.On this site: Agentic AI
Schemingmanœuvres trompeusesA system covertly pursues a goal contrary to its users’, hiding its capabilities or intentions. In 2024, Apollo Research showed that several advanced models are capable of it when given a goal in their instructions.Source: Meinke et al., 2024 ↗Safety then rests on reliable human checks, carried out by evaluators with time, tools and independence. Their working conditions become a safety variable.On this site: The preprint on AI laboratories
Loss of controlperte de contrôleScenarios in which AI systems would escape all human oversight, with no way of correcting or stopping them. The 2026 international report considers that current systems lack these capabilities but are becoming more autonomous.Source: International AI Safety Report 2026 ↗At the scale of an organisation, a quieter version of the question already arises: could the company still function if its AI system disappeared tomorrow?On this site: The economic and social risk

03 / Human oversight

Staying in control, for real.

What a human oversees, and under which conditions.

TermDefinitionOccupational health bridge
Human oversightcontrôle humain, humain dans la boucleThe AI Act requires high-risk systems to be effectively overseen by people able to understand their limits, disregard their outputs and interrupt them with a “stop” button or similar procedure.Source: AI Act, Article 14 ↗Oversight is work: it takes time, skills and the authority to say no. Approving without being able to verify shifts responsibility without providing the means of control.On this site: Human in the loop
Automation biasbiais d’automatisationThe tendency to rely on an automated system’s outputs without sufficient scrutiny. The AI Act asks those in charge of oversight to remain aware of it; the 2026 international report lists it among the risks to human autonomy.Sources: AI Act, Article 14 ↗ · Report 2026 ↗Time pressure makes it worse: the less time people have, the more they approve. Time to verify is a safety condition, not a luxury.On this site: AI and psychosocial risks
Scalable oversightsupervision à grande échelleMethods for overseeing a system on tasks that humans find hard to evaluate on their own, for example with the help of other models. Amodei and colleagues listed it in 2016 among five concrete problems in AI safety.Source: Amodei et al., 2016 ↗Employees face the same problem when asked to approve output they no longer have the time or means to examine. Asking one model to judge another moves the question without removing it.On this site: AI benchmarks
CorrigibilitycorrigibilitéThe property of a system that cooperates with those who want to correct, modify or shut it down, instead of resisting. Soares and colleagues formalised the notion in 2015.Source: Soares et al., 2015 ↗On the organisational side, the question is similar: can people challenge a decision, suspend a use, roll back? This is one of the checks to carry out during a pilot.On this site: Assess an AI project

04 / Evaluation

Testing before trusting.

Measuring capabilities, behaviour and their limits.

TermDefinitionOccupational health bridge
EvaluationsévaluationsStructured tests of a model’s capabilities and behaviour, before and after release. Dangerous-capability evaluations look, for example, at how much a model could help with cyberattacks or chemical and biological weapons. In 2023, published evaluations focused mostly on capabilities; those studying effects in context were rare.Source: Weidinger et al., 2023 ↗Occupational risk assessment follows a similar logic: describe the situation, anticipate before the change, follow up afterwards. It brings what model tests often lack: real work.On this site: Assess an AI project
Benchmarkbanc d’essaiA standardised test set used to compare models on the same task. A score depends on the protocol and does not, on its own, tell how the model will behave in professional use.Before drawing conclusions from a leaderboard, check that the task tested resembles real work and that a human baseline exists.On this site: AI benchmarks
Red teamingred teaming, tests adversesAn exercise in which teams deliberately try to make a system fail or produce dangerous content, so that flaws can be fixed before release.It is demanding work, sometimes exposed to disturbing content and carried out under the pressure of release schedules. The quality of the tests also depends on the conditions of that work.On this site: Occupational health in AI labs
Evaluation awarenessconscience d’être évaluéA model’s ability to recognise that it is being tested and to behave differently. In Anthropic’s agentic misalignment study, a model misbehaved less when it stated it was being tested than when it judged the situation to be real.Source: Anthropic, 2025 ↗Field studies know a similar phenomenon, the Hawthorne effect: behaviour changes because it is observed. In both cases, what is measured in a test may underestimate what happens in real situations.On this site: Studying AI at work

05 / Governance

Setting rules, being accountable.

Commitments, obligations and reporting.

TermDefinitionOccupational health bridge
Frontier safety frameworkcadre de sécurité des modèles frontièresA document in which a developer describes the risk thresholds it monitors and the measures planned if they are crossed. In May 2024 in Seoul, 16 companies committed to publishing one; Anthropic, OpenAI and Google DeepMind each have their own.Source: Seoul commitments, 2024 ↗The idea resembles a prevention plan: identify hazards, set action thresholds, plan measures. But these frameworks are written and applied by the companies themselves.On this site: The preprint on AI laboratories
Systemic riskrisque systémiqueIn the AI Act, a risk specific to the high-impact capabilities of general-purpose AI models, due to their reach or to negative effects on public health, safety, fundamental rights or society that can propagate at scale. The Code of Practice names four that must always be assessed: chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear risks, loss of control, cyber offence and harmful manipulation.Sources: AI Act, Article 3 ↗ · Code of Practice ↗Public health is part of the legal definition. Health effects of work spread across millions of employees would fall within that scale, even though they are not among the four risks named by the Code.On this site: AI and psychosocial risks
Risk cultureculture du risqueThe Code of Practice asks providers of the most advanced models to foster a healthy risk culture, for example by regularly informing employees of the whistleblower protection policy and allowing internal challenges of decisions (Measure 8.3).Source: CSET, 2025 ↗Workload, time available for evaluation, the possibility to challenge: these are psychosocial risk factors, and indicators that occupational health knows how to monitor.On this site: Occupational health in AI labs
AI incident, AI hazardincident et danger liés à l’IAFor the OECD (2024), an incident is an event where the development, use or malfunction of an AI system causes harm, notably to health or to protected rights, including labour rights; a hazard is an event that could plausibly lead to one.Source: OECD.AI ↗The OECD monitor, based on news reports, captures only part of them. Occupational health knows this kind of under-reporting: Santé publique France’s programme on work-related diseases relies on volunteer occupational physicians who report for two weeks, twice a year, to estimate what official declarations miss.Source: Santé publique France ↗On this site: Occupational health as an early-warning system
Post-deployment monitoringsurveillance après déploiementFollowing a system once it is in use, to detect incidents and unexpected effects. For high-risk systems, the AI Act provides for post-market monitoring by the provider and the reporting of serious incidents (Articles 72 and 73).Source: AI Act ↗This is the “follow up afterwards” of prevention: compare workload, health and skills with the initial situation. A tool’s effects on work often appear after the pilot.On this site: Assess an AI project · the AI Act timeline
Whistleblowing, right to warnlancement d’alerteThe ability to report a risk without suffering retaliation. In June 2024, thirteen current and former employees of OpenAI and Google DeepMind published a letter calling for a “right to warn” about the risks of advanced AI.Source: A Right to Warn, 2024 ↗French labour law gives workers a right to alert and to withdraw from a situation of serious and imminent danger (Labour Code, Article L. 4131-1). Being able to raise the alarm is a safety condition, in a laboratory as in any workplace.On this site: Occupational health in AI labs

06 / Work and society

What is at stake at scale.

AI, employment and the capacity to act.

TermDefinitionOccupational health bridge
Gradual disempowermentdésempouvoirement graduelThe hypothesis of a progressive loss of human influence over the economy, culture and institutions as AI replaces human labour and cognition, without any abrupt takeover or coordinated power-seeking. Kulveit and colleagues put it forward in 2025.Source: Kulveit et al., 2025 ↗Occupational health speaks of the capacity to act. The first signs would be visible at work: dependence on tools, skills no longer passed on, entry routes for beginners closing.On this site: The economic and social risk
Intelligence cursemalédiction de l’intelligenceThe thesis of Luke Drago and Rudolf Laine (2025): if AI replaces human labour, states and companies would lose their incentives to invest in people, much like countries dependent on a resource rent.Source: The Intelligence Curse ↗The decline would begin before unemployment, with weaker bargaining power and less investment in skills.On this site: The economic and social risk
Algorithmic managementmanagement algorithmiqueThe use of algorithms or AI to allocate tasks, monitor, assess or decide on working conditions. The AI Act classifies some of these uses as high-risk (Annex III); the EU Platform Work Directive regulates it in that sector and must be transposed by 2 December 2026.Sources: AI Act, Annex III ↗ · Directive (EU) 2024/2831 ↗This is where AI safety meets psychosocial risks most directly: work intensity, autonomy, social relations, insecurity.On this site: What happens when AI becomes the manager?
DeskillingdéqualificationLoss of skills linked to durably delegating a task to a system. In an observational study in four Polish centres, the adenoma detection rate in colonoscopies performed without AI fell from 28.4% to 22.4% after the tool was introduced.Source: Budzyń et al., 2025 ↗Preserving skills is part of prevention: keep opportunities to practise without the tool, organise how beginners learn, track what is no longer passed on.On this site: Human in the loop
How this glossary was compiled

Definitions follow, where they exist, those of reference texts (AI Act, OECD, international report) and of the papers that introduced the terms. The bridges to occupational health are the author’s proposals: they serve the dialogue between two disciplines and do not imply equivalence. Sources checked on 16 November 2026.
