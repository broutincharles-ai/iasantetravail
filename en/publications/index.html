Three publications to understand AI at work.

A preprint on the occupational health of the teams who evaluate and secure frontier AI models, an experimental study published in Références en Santé au Travail (INRS), and a narrative review of psychosocial risks associated with large language models. For each: the question, approach, findings and limitations.

01 / Preprint · Original article · October 2026

Occupational health as a condition for frontier AI safety: an analysis of 39 testimonies using the Gollac framework and of ten recent incidents at OpenAI and Anthropic.

Reference

C. Broutin
Preprint, version 1
Posted 4 October 2026

Preliminary version, not peer reviewed. Full text in English and French.

Read the preprint →Version française →

Study overview

This work analyses 39 testimonies from researchers and engineers at AI labs, published in the press between 2024 and 2026 and coded on the six dimensions of the Gollac framework, together with ten safety and security incidents documented at OpenAI and Anthropic.

Value conflicts appear in 31 of 39 testimonies, and 12 of the 28 testimonies describing impaired work quality concern safety work itself. Six of the ten incidents involve a work-related factor. The article discusses what the French occupational health system and EU law could offer.

It is a preprint based on press sources and company communications, without data collected from workers; it does not support causal inference between working conditions and model safety.

02 / INRS · Experimental study · June 2026

Artificial intelligence in occupational health: assessing its potential to improve the precision and clarity of medical recommendations.

Reference

C. Broutin
Références en Santé au Travail
No. 186 · June 2026
TF 335 · Pages 51–60

Research based on the occupational medicine residency thesis defended in Lille in October 2025.

Read on the INRS website ↗Full article · PDF in French ↗

Study overview

The study compared the o1 model with a multidisciplinary human reference on 385 medical recommendations, assessed against five wording criteria.

Across these criteria, 74.60% of assessments agreed with the human reference; 10.54% of disagreements were judged justified on review. The findings support an aid to professional review, with performance varying by criterion.

The experiment does not demonstrate improved worker health or validate autonomous medical decision-making. Results concern one model, one prompt and a specific corpus.

03 / Narrative review · 2025

Large language models and new psychosocial challenges at work: an occupational health challenge.

Reference

C. Broutin
Archives des Maladies Professionnelles et de l’Environnement
Volume 86, no. 4, article 102877 · August 2025

DOI: 10.1016/j.admp.2025.102877

Read the article ↗

Detailed summary based on the full seven-page article.

Study overview

This narrative review examines possible links between large language models, changes in work and psychosocial risks, using the six dimensions of the Gollac framework.

It discusses job insecurity, deskilling and loss of meaning, cognitive overload, and changes in working relationships. It proposes organisational prevention, training and worker participation.

It is a synthesis of literature and prevention proposals, not a cohort study or an experiment establishing a causal effect on health.
