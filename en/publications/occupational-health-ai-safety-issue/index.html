Occupational Health Is an AI Safety Issue

An editorial co-authored with Pouran D. Faghri, published ahead of print on 10 November 2026 in the Journal of Occupational and Environmental Medicine (JOEM). It argues that occupational health is part of AI safety and proposes a framework of six requirements.

Presented by Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician · Published online on 13 November 2026

02 / The thesis

Occupational health is part of AI safety.

As this site puts it.

In two sentences

By connecting work as it is actually done, organisation and health, occupational health already has analytical frameworks and preventive measures to detect AI-related changes in work early.

Its role should not begin when workers become unwell: it should begin when tools are chosen and work is redesigned.

Homepage, “Our thesis”

03 / The proposed framework

Six requirements for deploying AI without degrading work.

The framework proposed in the editorial, in the order this site presents it.

  1. Assess risks before deployment.
  2. Give workers an effective voice.
  3. Recognise hidden work.
  4. Ensure meaningful human control.
  5. Preserve skills and social support.
  6. Share benefits and monitor consequences.
Scope

This framework is proposed in the editorial; its own effectiveness remains to be evaluated. It is neither a validated method nor a regulatory standard.
