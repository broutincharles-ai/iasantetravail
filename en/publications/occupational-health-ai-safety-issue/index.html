Occupational Health Is an AI Safety Issue

Charles Broutin and Pouran D. Faghri

Journal of Occupational and Environmental Medicine · 10 November 2026

Editorial · Published ahead of print · DOI : 10.1097/JOM.0000000000003935

Read the editorial in the journal ↗

Abstract

Summary for this website.

Artificial intelligence (AI) safety also depends on the working conditions created by deployment. We argue for integrating occupational health into AI safety for two reasons: widespread psychosocial hazards could cause substantial population health harm; and losses of autonomy, skills, collective voice and economic security could signal a broader erosion of human agency, or human disempowerment.

Drawing on established psychosocial risk frameworks and the available evidence on AI at work, we examine work intensification, the burden of verifying AI outputs, and responsibility without effective control over decisions. Technically reliable AI can contribute to harmful work organisation, while productivity gains do not guarantee better health.

We propose six prevention requirements: assess risks before deployment, give workers a meaningful voice, account for hidden work, ensure meaningful human control, preserve skills and social support, and share benefits while monitoring consequences. Analysis of real work and repeated monitoring of psychosocial and organisational indicators should inform decisions to adapt or suspend use. This framework requires evaluation; the value of these indicators as early warnings of societal disempowerment remains a hypothesis. Training and equipping occupational health professionals could strengthen harm prevention and the early detection of concerning trajectories.
