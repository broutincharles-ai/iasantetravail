How macroeconomic indicators are evolving.

Firms, exposed jobs, hiring, layoffs: what do the statistics show about AI in the economy?

At the scale of the economy, the monitoring available shows no net effect of AI on employment. The first signals concern young people in the most exposed occupations. Each measure is given with its population, its date and its source.

By Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician · 22 November 2026

Two findings, and how they are changing.

Where the source gives the previous measure, it sits just below, as a hollow bar.

Spread and uses

One EU enterprise in five uses AI, up from 13.5% a year earlier. Worldwide, one worker in four is in an occupation exposed to generative AI.

Employment: early signals

At the scale of the economy, the monitoring available (OECD, ECB, Yale) shows no net effect of AI on employment. But hiring of young people is falling more in the most exposed occupations, in France as in the United States, although causality is not established. Not counted in these figures: the voluntary exit Microsoft offered to about 60,000 employees in October 2026.

Read these figures with care.

Announcements, not departures.

The count of layoffs attributed to AI rests on the reason the employer gives. A voluntary exit offer, like Microsoft’s, counts eligible employees, not departures.

Gaps, not causes.

Studies of young people’s employment compare more and less exposed occupations. Their authors describe a gap; they do not establish that AI is its cause.

Different scopes.

Each measure has its own population, country and date. The values cannot be added up or compared from one source to another.
