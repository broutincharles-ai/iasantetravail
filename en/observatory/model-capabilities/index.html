AI at Work Observatory

How model capabilities are evolving.

A point marked “40 hours” describes work that would take a human expert about 40 hours. The model has an estimated one-in-two chance of completing a task of that difficulty on its own.

The evaluation organisation METR measures the time an expert human needs for the tasks a model completes on its own half the time. Its update of 20 November 2026 covers fifteen public models from OpenAI and Anthropic.

By Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician · 22 November 2026

From 7 minutes to 40 hours.

Each point marks a model at its release date. A higher point means that the tasks it completes half the time would take a human expert longer.

Yesterday · May 2024

7 minutes

GPT-4o, the oldest of the models tested, completed tasks of this length half the time.

Today · September 2026

≈ 40 hours

The length measured for GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5: about 340 times that of May 2024. The uncertainty is great: from 13 to 2,645 hours for GPT-6 Astra.

Trend

×2 in 3.6 months

Doubling rate measured from May 2024 to September 2026. Models released since then and the laboratories’ internal models are not measured.

Strengths and weaknesses of current models.

Strong at coding.

On programming and optimisation tasks, models usually match or outperform skilled humans.

Performance drops when tasks are costly to reproduce for training.

On these tasks, the human working time corresponding to a one-in-two success rate was 2.9 times shorter. Cost here refers to building the training exercise.

Weaker at judging.

People did better in METR’s tests when the model had no way to check whether its answer was correct.

What the measure does not say.

Before citing a figure for an occupation, check the task tested, how the human working time was established and the uncertainty around the estimate.

A human duration.

The time horizon is the time an expert human needs for the task, not the time the model works on its own. A model is usually faster on the tasks it completes.

Mostly software tasks.

Programming, optimisation, machine learning, games and a few research tasks: the suite does not cover the variety of occupations. It measures tasks, not work as it is actually done.

An increasingly fragile measure.

Within a year the first set of tasks was too easy to distinguish the strongest models. For the new one, human time is usually estimated, not measured, and the confidence intervals of the latest models are very wide.
