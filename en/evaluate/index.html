Assess before. Test during. Review after.

Method principle

The questionnaire measures neither probability nor clinical severity. It helps identify situations to investigate with workers, their representatives and prevention professionals.

      03 / From results to decision

      The questionnaire opens the discussion. It does not close it.

      Six decisions must be understandable, discussable and traceable before the pilot, then reviewed after use.

      01

      Actual need

      What work-related problem are we trying to solve, for whom and with what expected result?

      02

      Scope

      What will the tool be able to do, what will remain human and which uses will remain excluded?

      03

      Data and version

      What data will flow and which exact version will actually be tested?

      04

      Oversight

      Who will review, with which skills, authority and available time?

      05

      Participation and appeal

      How will workers, their representatives and occupational health be involved?

      06

      Reversibility

      Which effects will trigger correction, suspension or termination of the project?

      Professional pilot

      Test on a small scale, observe the real work, retain the ability to reverse.

      1. Describe the baseline situation.
      2. Include ordinary cases and exceptions.
      3. Limit the scope and duration.
      4. Decide before any expansion.

      04 / Long-term follow-up

      Make follow-up a tool for managing work.

      A single snapshot is not enough. After the pilot, the same grid helps managers, HR, workers and prevention professionals jointly review workload, room for manoeuvre and actions taken.

      1. T0Before the pilotDescribe the use, baseline situation and anticipated risks.
      2. T14 to 8 weeksExamine the first observed facts and correct pilot conditions.
      3. T2At 3 monthsReview supervision workload, incidents and actual adoption.
      4. T3At 6 monthsReview organisation, skills, autonomy and open actions.
      5. T4+At 12 months, then every 6 to 12 monthsMaintain vigilance while the use, model or work continues to change.
      Monitoring log

      Keep what makes change interpretable.

      • a stable project reference, the system and its version;
      • the observed period and the team or occupations concerned;
      • changes in scope, objectives, staffing or organisation;
      • agreed actions, their owner, due date and status.
      Manager & HR safeguards

      Monitor work, never rank people.

      • do not use the profile to assess an individual worker or rank teams;
      • never convert NE or NA to zero or aggregate the nine axes into one total score;
      • involve users, their representatives and prevention professionals in interpretation;
      • reopen the assessment after a major change to the system or task, or after an incident.
      Also trigger a review

      Outside this schedule, complete a new assessment after a major change in model, data, objective, population or organisation — and whenever an incident or field signal warrants it. For shared HR use, this local tool does not replace a secure solution with access rights, defined retention and an audit log.

      05 / Method and limitations

      Nine dimensions, kept separate.

      The short online grid has 18 questions. The long instrument has 27 items — three per dimension — in two parallel T0/T1 forms and remains a separate development programme.

      01Work intensity and working time
      02Emotional demands
      03Autonomy and decision-making
      04Social relations and recognition
      05Value conflicts
      06Job insecurity
      07Opacity and contestability
      08Cognitive supervision workload
      09Skill erosion
      Tool status

      This grid is exploratory and non-psychometric. It is neither a diagnosis, proof of compliance nor a complete regulatory risk assessment.

      Each axis score is the mean of two numeric answers. It structures discussion and does not combine with the other axes into a total score.

      Answers are self-reported. They must be compared with observation of real work and the perspectives of the people concerned.
