9 October 2026 · 46th SFST Scientific Day · More than 330 participants

AI in occupational health at the SFST Scientific Day.

Just over 330 people followed the half-day devoted to AI in occupational health, held online as part of the 46th Scientific Day of the Société française de santé au travail (SFST, the French Society of Occupational Health): “Occupational health in the face of technological change? From the ecological transition to AI”.

From LLMs to agents, then psychosocial risks

I presented how LLMs work, their capabilities and limitations, then frontier models and AI agents, drawing in particular on a recent publication by the French Minister for Higher Education, Philippe Baptiste, to illustrate what it means for individuals when these models rival professional expertise.

I also briefly addressed the psychosocial risks associated with these uses and why occupational health professionals need to take an interest in them now.

One point seems essential to me: when an employee, an employer or an HR manager talks to us about “AI”, we should start by asking what they mean by it. A writing assistant, a decision-support tool and an agent able to carry out tasks autonomously do not transform work in the same way. The consequences for workload, autonomy or skills can be very different.

To explore these topics further: how LLMs work, agentic AI and AI and psychosocial risks.

The session programme

“The use of AI in occupational health services”, a session chaired by Prof. Bénédicte Clin-Godard (Caen) and Prof. Jean-François Gehanno (Rouen).

  1. 01

    “What are AI and large language models (LLMs)?”, the introduction I had the opportunity to give.

  2. 02

    A range of uses in occupational health: the literature review by Prof. Alexis Descatha (Angers), practical uses by Dr Marc Fadel (Angers) and digital twins by Dr Sara Trinh (Bourg-en-Bresse).

  3. 03

    The regulatory and professional ethics frameworks, by Cécile Théard-Jallu, lawyer (Paris), and Dr Sara Trinh.

  4. 04

    A conclusion on AI and the world of work, by Prof. Jean-François Gehanno (Rouen).

October 2026 · Lille Faculty of Medicine · Occupational medicine residents

A course on AI for occupational medicine residents.

I had the opportunity to give a course on artificial intelligence to the occupational medicine residents of the Lille Faculty of Medicine.

Psychosocial risks, uses in occupational health services and AI safety

I took the opportunity to raise their awareness of the psychosocial risks that can arise from its use at work, and to get them thinking about how AI is used and deployed in occupational health services.

I also addressed AI safety (cybersecurity, disempowerment…) and why occupational health has a role to play in it.

My thanks to Prof. Sophie Fantoni-Quinton for giving me this time.

To explore these topics further: AI and psychosocial risks, AI in occupational health services and AI safety.

September 2026 · Montpellier · Around 100 members

AI in occupational health: understanding, assessing and governing.

I would like to thank the Société régionale de médecine et d’hygiène du travail de Montpellier for inviting me to present artificial intelligence in occupational health to around one hundred of its members.

Understanding models and their implications

I discussed different models, their capabilities and limitations, frontier models and scaling laws, as well as their possible consequences for the economy and employment.

Identifying and preventing psychosocial risks

I also addressed the psychosocial risks associated with AI at work and what we can do, as occupational health professionals, to identify and prevent them.

Assessing uses in occupational health services

The presentation covered AI uses in occupational health services and how to conduct a pilot before deploying these tools internally, assessing their usefulness and their effects on real work.

The role of occupational health in AI safety

Finally, I discussed AI safety and why I believe occupational health has a role to play in this field.

16 January 2026 · ISTNF · Nearly 400 participants

The first Marcel Marchand Day dedicated to artificial intelligence.

On 16 January 2026, nearly 400 occupational physicians, residents and occupational health nurses attended the first Marcel Marchand Day dedicated to artificial intelligence. I had the honour of co-organising the event with ISTNF, at the initiative of Paul Frimat.

Supporting a major transformation of work

Artificial intelligence is gradually becoming part of the world of work and is expected to profoundly alter its balance in the years ahead. Recent months have already shown that it can have tangible and sometimes troubling consequences for workers: redundancies, rising unemployment in some sectors, changes to the very nature of occupations and transformations in working conditions.

This technological revolution also represents a major opportunity. Used appropriately, AI can become a genuine lever for improvement for employers and workers alike, for example by taking on certain repetitive tasks and allowing people to focus on higher-value activities.

Yet this potential can only be fully realised if these changes are properly supported. Occupational health professionals will have an essential role to play. We need to develop our expertise now so that we can anticipate, assess and prevent the emerging risks associated with these new uses as effectively as possible.

The afternoon programme

An initial opportunity to reflect and share perspectives across five themes.

  1. 01

    A general introduction to artificial intelligence and language models, presented by Dr Ramy Azzouz, hospital practitioner at Lille University Hospital and coordinator of the university diploma in AI and health.

  2. 02

    Lessons from practical applications of LLMs in occupational health through the example of RAVAIL – Travail Santé, presented by Dr Quentin Nocture and Pierre Flamant.

  3. 03

    An analysis of the psychosocial issues associated with the growth of LLMs and their implications for our discipline, which I had the opportunity to present.

  4. 04

    A presentation on using AI to detect and prevent musculoskeletal disorders, given by Axelle Savadogo, an occupational medicine resident.

  5. 05

    A broader reflection on the human dimension of this technological revolution, led by Charles Gorintin, co-founder of Alan as well as non-executive co-founder and board member of Mistral AI.

What comes next

I hope this first event met participants’ expectations and opened up new avenues for reflection. Further events on this topic are very likely to follow. In the meantime, I remain available to participants who would like to continue the discussion.

Acknowledgements

My warm thanks go to all participants and speakers, and to Stéphane Vandenbussche and Mustapha GHAMMADI for their involvement in organising the event.

21 October 2025 · French Ministry for the Economy and Finance · DGAFP

AI, work and mental health in the French civil service.

At the invitation of the DGAFP — the French Directorate-General for Administration and the Civil Service — I spoke at the Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty as part of the conference “Work and mental health: what challenges for the civil service?”, organised for the European Week for Safety and Health at Work.

A collective reflection

During a panel discussion with Valérie Bouvier and Fanny Pastant, moderated by Fabien Piazzon, I addressed the consequences of using artificial intelligence in the French civil service.

To explore these topics further: AI in occupational health services and AI and psychosocial risks.

Key points

The conditions required for an automation project to provide genuine support for work.

  1. 01

    A growing number of civil servants will see at least some of their tasks automated by artificial intelligence in the years ahead.

  2. 02

    This automation can disrupt how work is organised and may even profoundly transform its nature.

  3. 03

    These changes can contribute to a loss of meaning or autonomy, value conflicts, cognitive overload, hypervigilance or job insecurity: all well-established psychosocial risk factors.

  4. 04

    For now, artificial intelligence remains a tool. To become genuinely enabling, it must be adapted to people, not the other way round.

  5. 05

    Automation projects must be co-designed with managers, staff, social partners and occupational prevention and health services.

  6. 06

    Training and skills development are essential if staff are to understand and take ownership of these new tools.

  7. 07

    When a project is well designed and automation reflects the needs of the service and its staff, it can become a source of satisfaction and recognition at work. In particular, it can free up time for activities with greater human added value: creativity, decision-making, team cohesion and contact with the public.

  8. 08

    Given the challenges ahead for mental health and work retention, occupational prevention and health professionals must engage with these issues quickly so that they can advise and support managers and staff as effectively as possible.

Acknowledgements

I thank the DGAFP for inviting me to contribute to this discussion, and Valérie Bouvier, Fanny Pastant and Fabien Piazzon for the quality of our exchange.

November 2026 · Bulletin Joly Travail · Special report on AI systems

Artificial intelligence, occupational health and psychosocial risks.

I contributed to the special report “Law and practice of employment relations put to the test by AI systems”, published in the November 2026 issue of the French labour law journal Bulletin Joly Travail under the scientific coordination of Anaëlle Donnette.

Occupational health in a labour law report

My article presents artificial intelligence as a transformation of work whose effects on health depend on how it is used and on organisational choices.

It proposes the framework of the Gollac report as an analytical grid for occupational health professionals, so that the prevention of psychosocial risks is built into projects from the design stage.

The report brings together the views of academics, sociologists, lawyers and human resources practitioners.

Reference (in French): Broutin C. « L’intelligence artificielle, la santé au travail et les risques psychosociaux ». Bulletin Joly Travail, November 2026, no. 11, p. 48. Read on La Base Lextenso.

6 October 2026 · Les Echos · Press

AI at work and employees’ health, in Les Echos.

I would like to thank Les Echos, and Matthieu Quiret in particular, for giving me the opportunity to speak about the crucial subject of AI at work and its potential repercussions on employees’ health.

From employees to AI workers

Matthieu Quiret’s article is devoted to the “AI burnouts” now arriving in France. The mechanisms that contribute to psychosocial risks are detailed on this site.

But we should also bear in mind that the mental health of AI workers in turn affects the safety of the models.

Article (in French): Quiret M. « “Le sujet remonte très vite quand on gratte un peu” : les burn-out IA débarquent en France ». Les Echos, 6 October 2026. Read the article.

To explore these topics further: the mechanisms of AI-related psychosocial risks and occupational health as a condition for frontier AI safety.
