AI in OHS services: uses, risks and deployment method.

Author: Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician, AI lead of the SFST · Updated on September 11, 2026

In an occupational health service, artificial intelligence can assist with documentation, administration, prevention, member relations and certain strictly supervised medical uses. It replaces neither the analysis of work nor professional decision-making.

The common principle. Start with a specific task, use the minimum necessary data, measure errors and retain an identified person who can check the output. Présanse Charter, 2025 ↗

PST 5 · 2026–2030

What does PST 5 provide for AI at work?

The national plan makes AI at work a subject for prevention. In Objective 3, the Occupational Health Plan 2026–2030 requires AI-related transformations to be developed in consultation, respect working conditions and be evaluated through their real effects. Sub-action 3.1.1 provides for the creation of a national observatory for AI at work, led by the National Agency for the Improvement of Working Conditions (Anact).

01Study both sides.

PST 5 aims to document AI both as a tool for prevention and for improving working conditions, and as a possible source of occupational risks through AI systems and their uses.

02Produce useful knowledge.

The plan provides for quantitative and qualitative studies, experiments, training, webinars as well as tools and methods to move from findings to action.

03Give OHS services an explicit role.

Appendix 3 describes prevention and occupational health services as “pivotal players”. It asks them to contribute to knowledge about AI with Présanse and Anact, while developing a considered use of AI in their own activities.

For an occupational health service, the goal is therefore twofold: cautiously test well-defined professional uses and observe what AI deployment changes in the companies it supports — workload, autonomy, skills, work groups and occupational risks.

Source: Occupational health plan 2026–2030, action 3.1.1, p. 44–45, and annex 3, p. 154–156. Consult the official document ↗

02 / What already exists in France

Three examples from the field, three operational problems.

Présanse presented these experiences in November 2025. They document real approaches but do not, on their own, constitute evidence of generalisable effectiveness. Présanse article ↗

SPST 19-24 · Member relations

Absorb peaks in call volumes.

A callbot handles simple requests, responds or redirects. The human team remains available for complex situations and requests outside the defined scenario.

To measure: transfers, orientation errors, abandonments, satisfaction and rework burden.

Présanse field account ↗
MT71 · Administration

Streamline training administration.

An assistant handles pre-registrations, changes, communications with member companies and duplicate entry of information related to awareness sessions.

To measure: corrections, exceptions, end-to-end time and quality of exchanges.

Présanse field account ↗
AST74 · Governance

Create a dedicated AI function.

A cross-functional role drives the transformation, links strategy to the organisation and coordinates compliance, security and prioritisation.

To measure: documented decisions, training, incidents and real capacity to suspend a project.

04 / An example published by INRS

An assisted review evaluated on a clearly defined task.

This study, published by INRS in June 2026, evaluates the help provided by a language model without entrusting it with the consultation, the drafting of the opinion or the medical decision. INRS article ↗

In Charles Broutin’s study, “Artificial intelligence in occupational health: evaluation of its potential to strengthen the precision and clarity of medical recommendations”, the model achieved 74.6% overall agreement with the multidisciplinary consensus on 385 recommendations. Références en Santé au Travail, TF 335 ↗

Références en Santé au Travail · TF 335 · June 2026

The model only acts as a second reader.

The o1 model reviewed 385 medical recommendations that had already been drafted and contained no identifying information. It looked for five predefined defects: imprecision, ambiguity about an obligation, information outside Annex 4, an implicit job change, and disclosure of medical or personal data.

Essential limit. The study measures the detection of wording defects. It does not measure the quality of a consultation, nor the effect on health or continued employment.

06 / Shadow AI in OHS services

The risk exists before official deployment.

Présanse uses the term “Shadow AI” to describe uses that are neither validated nor supervised, including the risk of entering sensitive information into public chatbots. Présanse resource ↗

Weak signal, immediate risk

“I just wanted to save five minutes.”

A vague instruction, a personal tool and a copy and paste are enough for sensitive data to leave the intended framework. The Présanse charter prohibits the introduction of personal, medical or identifying data into a tool not validated by the service. Présanse good practices ↗

01
Map existing uses.

Understand practices, needs and workarounds without driving them underground.

02
Define prohibited data.

Give concrete examples for personal and health data.

03
Provide authorised alternatives.

Meet real needs with a framework that teams can actually use.

04
Train and document incidents.

Improve the framework based on observed errors and difficulties.

08 / Building a pilot in an OHS service

Need → value → data → risk → pilot → decision.

Présanse recommends starting small, measuring impacts, adjusting, then expanding gradually. Présanse approach ↗

01

Describe the need.

A recurring task, an observable problem and the professionals involved.

02

Estimate the value.

Expected quality, end-to-end time and benefit for the service's mission.

03

Audit the data.

Nature, necessity, access, conservation, hosting and minimisation.

04

Anticipate the error.

What can go wrong, who can see it and who can interrupt use.

05

Test in parallel.

Compare to a human reference without immediately modifying the usual circuit.

06

Decide collectively.

Continue, correct or stop based on documented results and effects.

Measure the system

What the tool does.

  • spotted errors, missed errors and invented responses
  • stability after a change of model or instruction
  • time saved after verification and correction
  • differences between cases, occupations and groups
Measure the work

How the tool affects teams.

  • checking workload, interruptions and exceptions
  • autonomy, the ability to challenge and real accountability
  • changes in skills and the work group
  • incidents, workarounds and inequalities in use

To prepare for this test, consult the assessment method before and after deployment, then use the short questionnaire on the effects of the AI project. To work through these concepts in order, follow the AI & Occupational Health pathway.

09 / Assign responsibilities

A service project, not a tool left to a few users.

The Présanse charter requires clear governance and the definition of who controls, validates and monitors uses. Principle of governance ↗

10 / AI training and literacy

Train each profession before deployment.

Useful training is not limited to operating a tool. It connects capabilities, limitations, data, verification, accountability and effects on real work.

Common foundation

Understand before use.

How models work, plausible errors, information protection, recourse, and human accountability.

Occupation-specific situations

Train on real tasks.

Use separate cases for occupational physicians, nurses, prevention professionals, assistants, management, the DPO and IT, with the unacceptable errors for each profession.

Organisation

Maintain the ability to control.

Verification time, right to challenge, reporting of incidents, updating of skills and shutdown conditions.

Article 4 of the AI Act requires providers and deployers to take measures to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy. The expected level depends in particular on knowledge, experience, training, the context of use and the people concerned. Read the European regulation ↗

11 / Direct answers

Six frequently asked questions about AI in OHS services.

These short answers summarize the decision points. Each statement refers to the corresponding source or detailed section.

What does “AI in OHS services” mean?

This means the use of artificial intelligence systems in prevention and occupational health services. AI can assist with administrative, documentary, prevention and member-relations tasks, as well as certain strictly supervised medical uses. It replaces neither the analysis of work nor professional decision-making. Read the full answer

Which uses of AI are relevant in an occupational health service?

The first candidates are precise and verifiable tasks: internal documentary research, administration of training, guidance of simple requests or preparation of documents. Medical and individual uses require greater vigilance, because the consequences of an error and the sensitivity of the data increase. Compare uses · Présanse ↗

Can we use consumer AI with health data?

No, not if the tool has not been authorised by the service. The Présanse charter requires that no personal, medical or identifiable data be entered into an unvalidated tool. The framework must specify hosting, access, purpose and responsibilities before any use. See the risk of Shadow AI · Présanse Charter ↗

How should an occupational health service choose its first AI pilot?

Choose a task with high occupational value and manageable risk. The pilot must start from an observable need, use the minimum amount of data, make errors visible and be capable of being stopped without disrupting the usual process. Test the matrix · Follow the six steps

Can AI decide on behalf of the occupational physician?

No. In the study published by INRS, the model acts as a second reader of an existing text; it does not conduct the consultation, make the decision or sign the recommendation. The occupational physician remains responsible for analysis, formulation and validation. Read the results and their limits · INRS TF 335 ↗

What should be measured during a pilot?

Measure both the system and the work. Monitor detected and missed errors, stability and end-to-end time after correction, as well as checking workload, autonomy, skills, incidents and inequalities in use. See the indicators · Consult the assessment method
