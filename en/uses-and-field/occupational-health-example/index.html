AI and SPSTI: uses, risks and deployment method.

Author: Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician, AI lead of the SFST · Updated on August 28, 2026

In an Inter-company Prevention and Occupational Health Service (SPSTI), artificial intelligence can assist with documentation, administration, prevention, member relations and certain strictly supervised medical uses. It does not replace either the analysis of the work or the decision of the professional.

The common principle. Start with a specific task, use the minimum necessary data, measure the errors and keep an identified person capable of controlling the output. Charte Présanse, 2025 ↗

PST 5 · 2026–2030

What does PST 5 provide for AI at work?

The national plan makes AI at work an occupational health priority. In its objective 3, the Occupational Health Plan 2026–2030 requires that transformations linked to AI be concerted, respectful of working conditions and evaluated based on their real effects. Sub-action 3.1.1 provides for the creation of a national observatory for AI at work, led by the National Agency for the Improvement of Working Conditions (Anact).

01Study both sides.

The PST 5 wants to document AI as a tool for prevention and improvement of working conditions, but also AI systems and their uses as possible sources of occupational risks.

02Produce useful knowledge.

The plan provides for quantitative and qualitative studies, experiments, training, webinars as well as tools and methods to move from findings to action.

03Give an explicit role to SPST.

Appendix 3 describes prevention and occupational health services as “pivotal players”. It asks them to contribute to knowledge about AI with Présanse and Anact, while developing a reasoned use of AI in their own activities.

For a SPSTI, the goal is therefore twofold: cautiously test well-defined professional uses and observe what the deployment of AI changes in the companies monitored — load, autonomy, skills, collectives and occupational risks.

Source: Occupational health plan 2026–2030, action 3.1.1, p. 44–45, and annex 3, p. 154–156. Consult the official document ↗

02 / What already exists in France

Three examples from the field, three operational problems.

Présanse presented these experiences in November 2025. They document real approaches but do not, on their own, constitute evidence of generalisable effectiveness. Présanse article ↗

SPSTI 19-24 · Member relations

Absorb call peaks.

A callbot handles simple requests, responds or redirects. The human team remains available for complex situations and non-scenario requests.

To measure: transfers, orientation errors, abandonments, satisfaction and rework burden.

Présanse Testimony ↗
MT71 · Administration

Streamline training administration.

An assistant handles pre-registrations, changes, communications with member companies and duplicate entry of information related to awareness sessions.

To measure: corrections, exceptions, end-to-end time and quality of exchanges.

Présanse Testimony ↗
AST74 · Governance

Create a dedicated AI function.

A cross-functional function drives the transformation, links strategy to the organisation and coordinates compliance, security and prioritisation.

To measure: traced decisions, training, incidents and real capacity to suspend a project.

05 / Shadow AI in SPSTI

The risk exists before official deployment.

Présanse uses the term “Shadow AI” to describe uses that are not validated or supervised, including the risk of introducing sensitive information into public chatbots. Présanse Support ↗

Weak signal, immediate risk

“I just wanted to save five minutes.”

A vague instruction, a personal tool and a copy and paste are enough for sensitive data to leave the intended framework. The Présanse charter prohibits the introduction of personal, medical or identifying data into a tool not validated by the service. Présanse best practices ↗

01
Mapping uses

Understand practices, needs and workarounds without making them clandestine.

02
Fix prohibited data.

Give concrete examples for personal and health data.

03
Propose solutions authorized.

Meet real needs with a framework usable by the teams.

04
Train and document incidents.

Advance the framework based on errors and difficulties observed.

07 / An example published by INRS

Assisted proofreading, evaluated on a clearly defined task.

This study, published by INRS in June 2026, evaluates the help provided by a language model without entrusting it with the consultation, the drafting of the opinion or the medical decision. INRS article ↗

In the study by Charles Broutin, “Artificial intelligence in occupational health: evaluation of its potential to strengthen the precision and clarity of medical recommendations ”, the model achieved 74.6% overall agreement with the multidisciplinary consensus on 385 recommendations. References in Occupational Health, TF 335 ↗

References in Occupational Health · TF 335 · June 2026

The model only acts as a second reader.

The o1 model reread 385 medical recommendations already written and devoid of identifying elements. It was looking for five defects defined in advance: imprecision, ambiguity on the obligation, information outside Annex 4, implicit change of position and disclosure of medical or personal data.

Essential limit. The study measures the detection of wording defects. It does not measure the quality of a consultation, nor the effect on health or continued employment.

08 / Building a pilot in SPSTI

Need → value → data → risk → pilot → decision.

Présanse recommends starting small, measure the impacts, adjust then gradually expand. Présanse approach ↗

01

Describe the need.

A recurring task, an observable problem and the professionals involved.

02

Estimate the value.

Expected quality, end-to-end time and benefit for the service's mission.

03

Audit the data.

Nature, necessity, access, conservation, hosting and minimisation.

04

Anticipate the error.

What can go wrong, who can see it and who can interrupt use.

05

Test in parallel.

Compare to a human reference without immediately modifying the usual circuit.

06

Decide collectively.

Continue, correct or stop based on documented results and effects.

Measure the system

What the tool does.

  • spotted errors, missed errors and invented responses
  • stability after change model or instructions
  • time saved after verification and correction
  • differences between cases, business and public
Measure the work

What the tool does for the teams.

  • control load, interruptions and exceptions
  • autonomy, contestation and real responsibility
  • evolution of skills and the collective
  • incidents, circumventions and inequalities of use

To prepare for this test, consult the evaluation method before and after deployment, then use the short questionnaire on the effects of the AI project. To take these concepts in order, follow the Workplace Health AI course.

10 / AI training and literacy

Train each profession before deployment.

Useful training is not limited to using a tool. It connects capabilities, boundaries, data, verification, accountability, and effects on actual work.

Common Core

Understand before you use.

How models work, plausible errors, information protection, recourse, and human accountability.

Situations profession

Train on real tasks.

Use separate cases for occupational physicians, nurses, prevention professionals, assistants, management, the DPO and IT, with the unacceptable errors for each profession.

Organisation

Maintain the ability to control.

Verification time, right to challenge, reporting of incidents, updating of skills and shutdown conditions.

Article 4 of the AI Act requires suppliers and deployers to take measures to ensure a sufficient level of mastery of AI. The expected level depends in particular on knowledge, experience, training, context of use and the people concerned. Consult the European regulation ↗

11 / Direct answers

Six frequently asked questions about AI and SPSTI.

These short answers summarize the decision points. Each statement refers to the corresponding source or detailed section.

What does “AI and SPSTI” mean?

This concerns the uses of artificial intelligence systems in inter-company prevention and occupational health services. AI can assist administrative, documentary, prevention, member relations tasks and certain strictly supervised medical uses. It does not replace either the analysis of the work or the decision of the professional. Read the full answer

Which uses of AI are relevant in a SPSTI?

The first candidates are precise and verifiable tasks: internal documentary research, administration of training, guidance of simple requests or preparation of documents. Medical and individual uses require greater vigilance, because the consequences of an error and the sensitivity of the data increase. Compare uses · Présanse ↗

Can we use consumer AI with health data?

No, not if the tool is not authorized by the service. The Présanse charter requires that no personal, medical or identifiable data be entered into an unvalidated tool. The framework must specify hosting, access, purpose and responsibilities before any use. See the risk of Shadow AI · Présanse Charter ↗

How to choose a first AI pilot in SPSTI?

Choose a strong business value with a manageable risk. The pilot must start from an observable need, use the minimum amount of data, make errors visible and be able to be stopped without disrupting the usual circuit. Test the matrix · Follow the six steps

Can AI decide on behalf of the occupational physician?

No. In the study published by INRS, the model acts as a second reader of an existing text; it does not conduct the consultation, make the decision or sign the recommendation. The occupational physician remains responsible for analysis, formulation and validation. Read the results and their limits · INRS TF 335 ↗

What should be measured during a pilot?

Measure both the system and the work. It is necessary to monitor the errors spotted or missed, the stability and the complete time after correction, but also the control load, autonomy, skills, incidents and inequalities of use. See the indicators · Consult the evaluation method
