OHS services · Professional uses · Human oversight

AI can support an OHS service when the task is precise.

Reviewing a document, finding a source, structuring observations or preparing training: value does not come from “AI” in general, but from a limited use that can be tested and corrected.

An evaluated example. A study published by INRS in June 2026 tested a language model as a second reader of occupational physician recommendations. It shows genuine potential, but not an ability to replace the occupational physician.

01 / Where AI may help

Six concrete uses with clearly defined human responsibility.

Except for the review case presented below, these uses are possibilities to experiment with and assess in each service.

01 · Occupational medicine Studied use

Review the wording of a recommendation.

Identify ambiguous wording, an implicit obligation, unnecessary information or a disclosure risk before transmission.

Humans retain: work-post analysis, final wording, decision and signature.
02 · All occupations Use to test

Query internal documentation.

Retrieve a protocol, risk sheet or procedure from a controlled repository, with sources shown in the answer.

Humans retain: verification of the source, its date and its applicability to the case.
03 · Prevention Use to test

Structure field observations.

Group notes by theme, prepare a synthesis and surface missing information before collective analysis.

Humans retain: observation, interpretation, prioritisation and proposed measures.
04 · Training Use to test

Prepare cases and materials.

Produce variants of a situation, discussion questions or an initial outline adapted to participants’ occupations.

Humans retain: objectives, content validity and teaching.
05 · Quality Use to test

Identify incomplete files.

Check for expected sections or flag a formal inconsistency without automatically assigning a risk level to the worker.

Humans retain: access to the file, interpretation and any resulting action.
06 · Administration Use to test

Reduce repetitive tasks.

Prepare a message, classify a non-medical request or rephrase practical information in more accessible language.

Humans retain: exception handling and validation before sending.
What must not be automated

Fitness for work, clinical direction, individual worker assessment or a prevention decision cannot be inferred from a model output without professional judgement and the work context.

02 / The case published by INRS

Test AI as a second reader, not as the author of the decision.

The study examines the ability of OpenAI’s o1 reasoning model to detect defects in recommendations that have already been written.

Références en Santé au Travail · TF 335

Evaluate a narrow task against a human reference.

The protocol evaluates neither an automated consultation nor end-to-end generation of a recommendation. The model only analyses supplied text against five predefined criteria.

Starting point. An earlier evaluation in Hauts-de-France classified 78% of recommendations as containing at least one of the five defects examined.

01
A multidisciplinary reference.The model’s answers are compared with recommendations previously assessed by multidisciplinary consensus.
02
A separately optimised prompt.Five iterations on one hundred recommendations, then a new test on fifty examples before final analysis.
03
An independent final sample.The validated prompt is applied to 385 newly and randomly selected recommendations.
04
Four possible outcomes.Agreement, justified divergence after reassessment, over-detection or an error missed by the model.
05
Texts without identifiers.The recommendations provided contained no information identifying the worker, company, employer, physician or originating service.

03 / What the model looked for

Five wording defects defined before analysis.

The model was not asked to invent a better recommendation. It had to flag the possible presence of specific problems.

CriterionProblem examinedExample point of concernWhy it matters
01ImprecisionDifficulty for the employer to understand or apply the recommendation.Undefined duration, missing frequency, unclear end point or “renewable” without a schedule.An unclear instruction may be misapplied and undermine job retention.
02Uncertainty about obligationWording suggesting that a measure is optional or negotiable.Conditional wording, unjustified alternative or choice introduced into the opinion.The binding force of the recommendation must be understood without ambiguity.
03Information outside Annex 4Comment or exchange with the employer that does not constitute an adjustment measure.General information without a concrete recommendation.The document must remain focused on measures necessary for the work.
04Disguised job changeRecommendation for another job or a clearly disproportionate adjustment.A measure that effectively removes the worker from their previous post.A recommendation must not substitute for an appropriate medical conclusion.

04 / Results

High overall agreement, but substantial variation by criterion.

The average must not hide the differences: the model is highly reliable for some defects and much more debatable for others.

The appropriate conclusion

The o1 model appears promising as a review tool. The results show neither that it can write a recommendation independently nor that it already improves health or job retention.

05 / Limits

What the study does not establish.

Transparency about these limits prevents an encouraging result from becoming a general claim about all models or uses.

01

The work context was missing.

The evaluators and model lacked detailed information about the work post, worker or company-specific constraints.

02

Only one model was studied.

The results concern o1 under the precise conditions of this study. They cannot be directly extrapolated to other models.

03

Performance changes rapidly.

Newer models may outperform o1 on certain tests. A protocol must therefore be rerun when the system or prompt changes.

04

No clinical effect was measured.

The study assesses defect detection, not actual employer understanding, implementation of the measure or job retention.

Zero hallucinations does not mean zero risk

No hallucination was observed in this sample because the task and prompt were tightly constrained. Another model, prompt or text may produce different errors.

06 / Build a pilot in an OHS service

Evaluate the use before trying to scale it.

A useful pilot begins with a measurable question and a human reference. It must also allow a return to the previous way of working.

01

Choose a narrow task.

A recurring, correctable problem distinct from the clinical decision.

02

Establish the reference.

A set of cases assessed by professionals using explicit criteria.

03

Protect the data.

Minimisation, appropriate hosting, limited access and no unnecessary identifiers.

04

Test in parallel.

The model acts as a second reader without changing the usual process.

05

Measure errors.

Agreement by criterion, over-detections, missed defects, time saved and new review workload.

06

Decide collectively.

Continue, correct or stop based on documented results and effects on work.

Measure performance

What the system does.

  • errors detected and missed
  • differences by occupation and case
  • stability after a model or prompt change
  • time actually saved, including corrections
Measure the work

What the system does to teams.

  • review workload and interruptions
  • autonomy and ability to challenge
  • changes in skills and learning
  • incidents, workarounds and unequal use

07 / Allocate responsibilities

A service-wide project, not a tool left to a few users.

Professionals must be able to define the criteria, limits and situations in which the system must not be used.

Physicians and nurses
ContributionDefine cases, the professional reference and unacceptable errors.
ResponsibilityRetain clinical interpretation and validation of every output used.
Risk prevention specialists, ergonomists and psychologists
ContributionDescribe work situations and the pilot’s organisational effects.
ResponsibilityPrevent an automated synthesis from replacing field analysis.
Assistants and administrative teams
ContributionIdentify repetitive tasks, exceptions and information needs.
ResponsibilityReport the actual correction workload and errors affecting service users.
Leadership, DPO and IT
ContributionOrganise the contract, data, access, security and monitoring.
ResponsibilityProvide the means to stop use and inform the relevant bodies.
Works council and employee representatives
ContributionExamine consequences for occupations, workload, autonomy and skills.
ResponsibilityRequest a documented review before any wider rollout.
