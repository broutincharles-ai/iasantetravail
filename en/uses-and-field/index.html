Field use · Deployment support

Deploy AI without discovering its effects after the fact.

Define the use before implementation, organise a reversible pilot, then compare promises with effects actually observed in the work.

Why a questionnaire? To make this approach concrete, I developed an exploratory tool based on the six families of psychosocial risk factors defined in the Gollac report, supplemented by three AI-specific dimensions. It structures discussion and highlights points requiring attention. Its effectiveness and measurement properties have not yet been scientifically demonstrated.

02 / Before deployment

Six decisions to make before launching a pilot.

The questionnaire does not replace collective decision-making. It makes explicit the choices too often buried in a vendor presentation or productivity promise.

DecisionQuestion to resolveRecord to keep
01Actual needWhat work problem are we trying to solve, for whom and with what expected outcome?Description of the current task and project objective
02ScopeWhat the tool may do, what will remain human, and which uses are explicitly excluded.Authorised use cases, limits and population concerned
03Data and versionWhich data enters the system, where it flows, and which exact version will be tested?Sources, retention rules and version record
04ReviewWho will review the outputs, with what skills, information and available time?Named owner, review procedure and planned workload
05Participation and appealHow will workers, their representatives and occupational health be involved? Who can challenge an output?Participation, reporting and appeal arrangements
06ReversibilityWhich errors or effects will trigger correction, suspension or termination of the project?Decision thresholds and fallback to the previous way of working
The record that matters

At the end of project definition, everyone should understand what will and will not be tested, who decides, and under which conditions the experiment can be stopped.

03 / Organise the pilot

A pilot is not a demonstration under favourable conditions.

It tests a hypothesis in real work, including incomplete information, atypical cases and time pressure.

01 · Baseline

Describe the situation without the tool

Quality, lead times, workload, incidents, room for manoeuvre and existing difficulties. Without this baseline, no gain can be attributed to the project.

02 · Test cases

Include ordinary work and exceptions

Test frequent, rare, ambiguous and potentially serious cases, as well as situations in which the system should recognise that it cannot answer.

03 · Limited conditions

Set a duration and scope

Limit the population, data and decisions concerned. Maintain a fallback and avoid irreversible dependency during the trial.

04 · Decision gate

Review before expanding

Bring together results, user experience and occupational health signals to decide whether to continue, modify, suspend or abandon the project.

What a vendor demonstration cannot measure.

  • Time required to review and correct
  • Additional work created by exceptions
  • Effects on mutual support and skills
  • The practical ability to challenge an answer
  • Errors specific to your data and occupation
  • How objectives change once the tool is installed

04 / After deployment

Track effects hidden by an average performance figure.

An overall improvement can hide workload shifted onto certain occupations, loss of autonomy or rare but serious errors. Follow-up must therefore combine measures with field feedback.

DimensionWhat to observeSignal requiring action
01Final quality

Errors, omissions, rework, incidents prevented or created, and variation between cases.

A serious, repeated or hard-to-detect error.
02Time and workload

Production time, but also review, correction, learning and exception-handling time.

Review cancels out the gain or is performed under time pressure.
03Autonomy and appeal

Ability to depart from the output, explain a decision and obtain a review.

The recommendation becomes an instruction that cannot be challenged.
04Skills

Opportunities to learn, maintenance of independent practice and ability to identify an incorrect output.

People can no longer act without the tool or check its output.
05Work collective

Task allocation, cooperation, knowledge transfer and clarity of responsibilities.

Difficult work becomes concentrated among a few people or becomes invisible.
06Warnings and health

Reported difficulties, fatigue, tensions, sickness absence and situations in which use must be interrupted.

Warnings increase without a response or a way to stop.

05 / Use the tool in your organisation

A shared basis for discussion, not a score imposed on people.

The questionnaire can be used directly. For a collective approach, a pilot or adaptation to your context, an initial discussion helps select the right scope and participants.

01

Employers and executives

Document the decision, resources, responsibilities, pilot conditions and criteria for continuing or stopping.

02

HR and transformation teams

Examine redesigned tasks, actual workload, skills to preserve, training and employment consequences.

03

Employee representatives and works councils

Use a shared framework to examine working conditions, request relevant information and agree follow-up.

04

OHS services and prevention professionals

Become involved during project definition, identify exposure situations and help choose collective prevention indicators.

06 / Method & limits

What the questionnaire can do — and what it does not claim to do.

The tool’s value depends on the quality of the discussion and information entered. Its result is neither a compliance label nor automatic authorisation to deploy.

01

Exploratory tool

It structures analysis and highlights points requiring attention. It has not been validated as a psychometric test.

02

No identifying data

The questionnaire asks for no person, company or provider names. Answers remain on the device.

03

No worker scoring

The profile concerns a project and work situation. It must not be used to rank or individually assess workers.

04

No substitute

It replaces neither prevention duties, social dialogue nor specialised legal, clinical or technical analysis.
