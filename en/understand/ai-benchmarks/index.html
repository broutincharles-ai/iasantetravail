Understanding AI at work / Short page

What is an AI benchmark for?

A benchmark is a standardised test. An AI model gets a set of tasks and its answers are scored using a rule set in advance. Giving the same test to two models, or two versions of one model, helps you choose which to try for your work.

By Dr Charles Broutin, occupational physician · Updated on 27 November 2026

See three examples ↓Read the full chapter →

Three questions you can test

Suppose your team is choosing between two tools. What you ask them to do depends on the work you have in mind.

  • Find an instruction. Give two models the same ten fictional procedures. For each one, ask who should receive an incident report. A model that names the right recipient eight times scores 8/10 on this test.
  • Summarise a meeting. Give both models the same meeting notes. Check which one preserves each decision, its owner and its deadline. Record any invented decision separately, however readable the summary is.
  • Choose a message. Ask colleagues to read two anonymous drafts. Their vote tells you which they prefer. To check whether the time in the message is accurate, compare it with the schedule.

01 / Overview

Five families of benchmarks

These families can overlap. An occupation-specific test can also measure preference if professionals vote for their favourite answer.

FamilyWhat the test asksHow to interpret the result
KnowledgeThe model picks one answer from several options. MMLU and GPQA count correct answers.If the questions appeared in its training data, the model may have memorised them.
Real tasksThe model produces work that can be checked. In SWE-bench it proposes a fix for a software issue and tests check whether the fix works. The Remote Labor Index examines professional projects.Fixing the issue tells you little about other parts of the job, such as establishing what a client needs.
Human preferenceIn LMArena, a person reads two answers without seeing which models produced them and votes for the one they prefer.A clear answer can win the vote even if it contains an error.
Autonomous behaviourAn agent selects and carries out several actions to reach a goal. METR measures its success rate on tasks grouped by how long a human expert takes to complete them. In Vending-Bench it manages a simulated vending machine.Check which tools, time limits and permissions the agent had. Changing them can change the outcome.
Occupation-specific testsThe cases and scoring criteria come from a particular field. MedHELM covers medical tasks. BenchMedTrav, designed by this site’s author, covers occupational health.A team still needs to check whether the cases resemble its own and whether it has time to review the answers.

Two precautions

The gaps between the leaders are often smaller than the measurement uncertainty. And the score depends on the protocol: on ARC-AGI-3, with each provider’s own set-up, four models exceed 97%.

03 / Method

Four questions before drawing a conclusion.

To ask of any score, whatever the test.

01 · Task

Does the test resemble the intended work?

Same kinds of tasks, same level of difficulty, same documents as those the tool will handle?

02 · Protocol

Under what conditions?

Which prompt, which tools, how many attempts and which model version?

03 · Measure

How is the score calculated?

Accuracy, voter preference, cost, speed or something else?

04 · Fragility

What does the average hide?

Could the questions have been seen during training? Which errors, perhaps serious ones, disappear into the overall figure?

04 / At work

How do you move from a ranking to a workplace trial?

A good technical score does not, by itself, predict good professional use.

Before a trial with your team. Choose two or three fictional cases that resemble your work, including a difficult one. Give the shortlisted models the same instructions. Record omissions, serious errors and correction time. With the people who will review the work, decide whether a tool warrants a wider trial.

The model

Does the system answer correctly?

Check each summary against the original minutes. A missing deadline and an invented decision need different corrections. Record them separately even if both models have the same average score.

The deployment

Is the work done better without creating new risks?

During the trial, compare drafting and review time with the time the task used to take. If errors reach recipients, ask the team where the check failed and whether sending should pause.

Perceived gain, measured gain

In a randomised trial run by METR in early 2025 with 16 experienced developers, they believed they were 20% faster with AI; they were measured 19% slower. A new measurement in late 2025 suggests a speed-up instead, which METR considers weak evidence. Becker, Rush, Barnes et al., METR, 2025 ↗

Key answers

Frequently asked questions

Which is the best AI model?

There is no single answer: the leader changes with the test, and the gaps between the leaders are often smaller than the measurement uncertainty. The useful question is which model succeeds at your own tasks.

What is benchmark contamination?

It is when a test’s questions end up in the models’ training data. Multiple-choice knowledge tests are highly exposed to it: a good score may then come from questions already seen.

Are there benchmarks for occupational health?

Tests built by professionals in the field are the closest to the job: MedHELM in medicine, or BenchMedTrav in occupational health, designed by the author of this site. Even with these, real deployment still has to be assessed.
