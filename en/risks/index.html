Risks · Prevention · Two scales of analysis

AI-related risks at work need to be understood at two scales.

What a system changes in work and health today; what the accumulation of those changes could alter tomorrow in employment, economic power and social cohesion.

The risk does not lie in the technology alone. It depends on the role assigned to the system, the tasks and objectives, responsibilities, the scope for oversight, and how the organisation evolves around its use.

01 / Two complementary perspectives

One transformation, two levels of risk.

The two analyses complement each other, but differ in focus, time horizon and evidentiary status. The first starts from a specific use case; the second considers what wider adoption could transform.

02 / Shared reference points

Six shared areas of concern link the two analyses.

A single deployment may produce both favourable and adverse effects. At either scale, the task is to understand what changes, for whom, for how long, and with what real ability to understand, challenge or correct it.

  • 01WorkloadWork intensification, deadlines and invisible work.
  • 02PowerAutonomy, monitoring and the ability to challenge decisions.
  • 03AccountabilityBeing answerable for a decision while retaining control over it.
  • 04SkillsLearning, knowledge transfer and dependency.
  • 05EquityBias, exposure and inequalities.
  • 06FutureEmployment, economic power and social cohesion.

03 / Connecting the scales

Systemic risk can begin in everyday work.

Long before it appears in national statistics, an economic transformation may surface through ordinary organisational changes. These signals do not prove that a systemic shift is under way; they show what needs to be made visible and discussed collectively.

Organisation

Producing more with smaller teams.

Positions left unfilled when people leave, higher targets, more checking work and tasks progressively transferred to systems.

Career pathways

Weakening pathways for learning.

Fewer entry-level roles, fewer opportunities to practise skills and a gradual breakdown in knowledge transfer.

Agency

Remaining accountable while becoming dependent.

A recommendation becomes the default decision, while the human capacity to understand, challenge or regain control diminishes.

Occupational health cannot predict the future capabilities of models. It can, however, observe what their deployment actually does to work and bring individual signals to the collective level.
