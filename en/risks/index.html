OCCUPATIONAL RISKS · REAL WORK · PREVENTION

What are the risks of artificial intelligence at work?

Risk does not come from the technology alone. It appears when its use transforms workload, decision-making latitude, monitoring, responsibilities, skills or job security without the means of control and regulation changing accordingly.

AI can improve how a task is performed while degrading the conditions in which it is carried out.

The main risks concern work intensification, the burden of verification, reduced autonomy, monitoring, the opacity of certain decisions, skill erosion, inequalities and professional uncertainty.

Their significance depends on the use case, the role given to the system, the duration of exposure and the real possibilities of understanding, challenging or correcting how it works.

01 / MAP

Six families of risk to examine together.

A single use can affect several dimensions of work at once. Analysis must therefore cover the work situation as a whole rather than a succession of risks considered separately.

01 / WORKLOAD

Intensification and invisible work

Raised targets, shortened deadlines or an increased volume of output may come with new checking, correction and coordination tasks that sometimes remain barely visible.

02 / POWER

Autonomy, monitoring and contestation

A recommendation, a score or an automated decision can become difficult to challenge when its criteria are opaque or when the avenues of appeal remain limited.

03 / ACCOUNTABILITY

Answering for a decision without controlling it

A person may remain responsible for a result produced with the help of a system without having the time, the information, the skills or the authority needed to verify its reliability.

04 / OCCUPATION

Skills, learning and meaning at work

Automating certain tasks can reduce the opportunities to practise, understand and pass on know-how, to the point of creating a gradual dependence on the tool.

05 / EQUITY

Bias, data and inequalities

A system’s errors or performance differences can unequally affect certain occupations, populations or situations that are insufficiently represented in the data.

06 / FUTURE

Employment and job security

Uncertainty about how tasks, occupations, assessment criteria or the value of skills will change can produce effects well before any job is actually cut.

03 / PREVENT

Act on the organisation before asking people to adapt.

Training is necessary, but it cannot compensate for a poorly designed organisation, unrealistic targets, checking without dedicated time, insufficient avenues of appeal or disproportionate monitoring.

01 —

Describe

Specify the task, the people concerned, the data used, the role given to the system, the objectives pursued and the baseline situation.

02 —

Limit

Define excluded uses, plan a reversible deployment, guarantee the possibility of departing from the system, and set stopping criteria as well as a fallback option.

03 —

Provide the means

Provide the time, information, skills, support and authority needed to check, correct or suspend use of the system.

04 —

Monitor

Observe over time the effects on quality, workload, autonomy, skills, work teams, incidents and any signals of deteriorating health.
