# Am I leaving humans with only the hardest interactions?

ID: Q06 | Record: R06 | Version: 1.1.0 | Date: 2026-09-13
Reference: https://www.iasantetravail.com/agents/#Q06
Publisher: IA & Santé au Travail — https://www.iasantetravail.com/

## Answer

Check what remains after automation. Handling fewer cases can still be more demanding when routine interactions disappear and people receive a concentrated stream of distress, conflict, or unresolved problems.

Review case mix and exposure duration with the team. Adjust allocation for complexity and emotional demands, preserve access to experienced colleagues, and make room for recovery after difficult interactions.

## Example — hypothetical

Twenty routine calls and twenty escalated complaints are not equivalent workloads simply because the counts match.

## Before acting, check
- Case mix and emotional demands before and after routing changes.
- Exposure duration and opportunities for recovery or rotation.
- Available peer support and procedures for difficult incidents.

## Options to compare
- Balance assignments by complexity and emotional demand.
- Preserve recovery, team support, and access to experienced colleagues.

## What to follow up
- Consecutive difficult cases and incident reports.
- Availability of support and perceived emotional burden.

## What makes the situation more protective
Routing includes complexity, emotional exposure, and access to support.

## Ask for human review when
Case volume is the only workload measure or difficult exposure concentrates on a few workers.
