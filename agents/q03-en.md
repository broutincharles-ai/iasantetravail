# Is asking a human to approve my work enough?

ID: Q03 | Record: R03 | Version: 1.1.0 | Date: 2026-09-13
Reference: https://www.iasantetravail.com/agents/#Q03
Publisher: IA & Santé au Travail — https://www.iasantetravail.com/

## Answer

Meaningful review requires access to the relevant evidence, the competence and time to examine it, and the authority to reject or change the result. Identify who can actually stop or correct the process.

Match the volume of outputs to the available review capacity. If reviewers cannot realistically check what reaches them, reduce the scope or pace, improve the supporting evidence, or ask the responsible person to revise the arrangement.

## Example — hypothetical

A reviewer expected to approve 40 complex outputs in ten minutes may have a formal role but insufficient review capacity. Verify the task demands before relying on the approval.

## Before acting, check
- Output volume, error types, and realistic review time.
- Access to original evidence and competence to verify it.
- Authority to reject, stop, and obtain support.

## Options to compare
- Limit throughput to available review capacity.
- Give reviewers evidence access, escalation support, and stop authority.

## What to follow up
- Review backlog and time available per case.
- Errors found after approval and rejected outputs.

## What makes the situation more protective
The reviewer can understand, challenge, correct, and suspend the system in practice.

## Ask for human review when
Responsibility exceeds available authority or checking capacity.
