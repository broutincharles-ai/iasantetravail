# Am I helping the team cooperate—or pushing people to compete?

ID: Q08 | Record: R08 | Version: 1.1.0 | Date: 2026-09-13
Reference: https://www.iasantetravail.com/agents/#Q08
Publisher: IA & Santé au Travail — https://www.iasantetravail.com/

## Answer

Check what your objectives and allocation rules reward. If helping colleagues, mentoring, and coordinating reduce an individual’s score, your system may discourage work the team needs.

Account for those collective contributions in workload and recognition. Watch who inherits the complex cases and who provides unrecorded support. Compare team-level outcomes and workers’ experience alongside individual output.

## Example — hypothetical

A colleague who handles fewer cases because they train three newcomers may be making a substantial contribution that a simple output ranking misses.

## Before acting, check
- How coordination, assistance, and mentoring are recognized.
- Distribution of complex tasks and access to expert support.
- Shared working time and opportunities to discuss problems.

## Options to compare
- Include necessary collective contributions in workload planning.
- Protect shared review time and rotate demanding assignments where feasible.

## What to follow up
- Time devoted to assistance and mentorship.
- Concentration of difficult work and unanswered requests for support.

## What makes the situation more protective
The organization recognizes collective work and provides time and resources for it.

## Ask for human review when
Individual gains rely on unrecognized work transferred to colleagues.
