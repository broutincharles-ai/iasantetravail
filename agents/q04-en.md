# How much should I monitor the people I manage?

ID: Q04 | Record: R04 | Version: 1.1.0 | Date: 2026-09-13
Reference: https://www.iasantetravail.com/agents/#Q04
Publisher: IA & Santé au Travail — https://www.iasantetravail.com/

## Answer

Start with the operational problem you need to understand, then use the least intrusive information sufficient to address it. A team-level bottleneck may be identifiable without continuous individual tracking.

If individual information is necessary and authorized, make its purpose and use understandable, check that it represents the work, and allow factual errors to be corrected. Include contributions that activity traces miss, such as helping colleagues, preparing difficult cases, or preventing errors.

## Example — hypothetical

Low keyboard activity could reflect a useful conversation with a colleague. It is insufficient evidence, by itself, of low contribution.

## Before acting, check
- What is collected, for what purpose, and who can access it.
- Whether the metric captures actual work and necessary non-measured tasks.
- Worker input, correction procedures, and how the metric affects decisions.

## Options to compare
- Use the least intrusive information sufficient for the operational need.
- Review collective bottlenecks before attributing problems to individuals.

## What to follow up
- Monitoring-related complaints and disputed records.
- Unrecorded support work and avoidance of necessary low-scoring tasks.

## What makes the situation more protective
Measurement is proportionate to a clear purpose, contextualized, and open to meaningful challenge.

## Ask for human review when
A narrow activity proxy is treated as a complete measure of contribution.
