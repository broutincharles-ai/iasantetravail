# How should I talk about changes to roles and jobs?

ID: Q10 | Record: R10 | Version: 1.1.0 | Date: 2026-09-13
Reference: https://www.iasantetravail.com/agents/#Q10
Publisher: IA & Santé au Travail — https://www.iasantetravail.com/

## Answer

Separate confirmed decisions from scenarios and unresolved questions. Be explicit about what you know, what you do not know, who decides, and when people can expect an update. A task’s exposure to automation does not establish that a job will disappear.

Explain concrete changes in responsibilities and available training or transition support. Give workers and their representatives a meaningful way to influence implementation. Make commitments only within the authority and resources actually available.

## Example — hypothetical

“We have not decided how roles will change; the project lead will discuss the options with the team next month” is appropriate only if that process and timing are actually agreed.

## Before acting, check
- Confirmed decisions, open questions, and the timetable for resolving them.
- Expected task changes and available training or transition support.
- Ways for workers and representatives to influence implementation.

## Options to compare
- Communicate what is decided, what is uncertain, and when updates will occur.
- Co-develop role changes and practical learning or transition routes.

## What to follow up
- Unanswered role questions and access to training.
- Worker-reported uncertainty and participation in change decisions.

## What makes the situation more protective
The organization makes honest commitments, updates information, and provides meaningful participation and support.

## Ask for human review when
A plan makes unsupported predictions about job loss or offers reassurance without an implementation plan.
